# Sources and adaptations

These sources inform the skill. They do not prescribe a single explanation
format or establish that the skill itself works; judge that through use.

- [Bret Victor: Up and Down the Ladder of Abstraction](https://worrydream.com/LadderOfAbstraction/).
  Connect concrete behavior with broader views. Our application is to move
  between an example and a design decision when that helps the reader reason.
- [Schaffer and colleagues: Explainers' Mental Representations of Explainees' Needs in Everyday Explanations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.08514).
  This qualitative study of nine explainers explores how they represent a
  listener's developing knowledge and interests. It motivates an audience model
  that changes through conversation; its small study does not establish a
  universal explanation method.
- [Fiorella and Mayer: Principles for Reducing Extraneous Processing in Multimedia Learning](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/cambridge-handbook-of-multimedia-learning/principles-for-reducing-extraneous-processing-in-multimedia-learning/F29A19FCD34C542806F736E0661C05F5).
  Coherence, signaling, and spatial contiguity inform the emphasis on relevant
  content, visible structure, and labels close to their subjects. Applying those
  learning principles to software briefings is a design choice here.
- [Simon Brown: C4 dynamic diagrams](https://c4model.com/diagrams/dynamic).
  Use a view of one runtime interaction when the question concerns how parts
  collaborate. The skill leaves notation and diagram type open.
- [Edward Tufte: Design of causal diagrams](https://www.edwardtufte.com/notebook/design-of-causal-diagrams-barr-art-chart-lombardi-diagrams-evolutionary-trees-feynman-diagrams-timelines/).
  Examine the claims that connecting lines imply. Our application is to give
  software-diagram arrows a clear meaning supported by the implementation.
- Brandon's named models for explanation: Steve Jobs's
  [Thoughts on Flash](https://newslang.ch/wp-content/uploads/2022/06/Thoughts-on-Flash.pdf)
  for a plain argument with a clear position; Richard Feynman, Gerald Sussman,
  and Andrej Karpathy for simple ideas shown through concrete cases; Jakub
  Krehel's "less is more" for spare interfaces and copy; William Zinsser for
  simplicity, brevity, clarity, and humanity.
- Brandon's Claude and Codex history, read on 3 November 2026. About ninety
  places where Brandon pushed back on an explanation supplied the failure
  patterns and the examples. The private-vocabulary failure in status updates
  appeared more often than any other.
